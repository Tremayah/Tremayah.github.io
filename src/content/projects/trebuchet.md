¶ **Lead — what the thing is.** A laser-cut catapult that fires a small metal ball at laser-cut building blocks from a set range. Says in one breath what the project was really about: market competition, laser-cutting technique, moving parts, game design.

¶ **The shape of the four weeks.** Iterating through prototypes and experiments — kerf-hinge joints, laser rasterisation, precise drawing in Rhino 8.

## The brief

¶ **The brief as set.** Design and prototype a laser-cut toy or game from a single 760 × 440 mm sheet of laser ply, using sustainable design principles — restricted materials, recyclability, repairability, energy in manufacture, distribution and use.

## Crush the Castle

¶ **Where the idea came from.** The Flash game played at the back of the class in the school computer suite — a catapult, a variety of projectiles, a castle to topple and a king to defeat, in the mould of Angry Birds.

<section class="proj-row proj-row--rev">
  <figure class="proj-media">
    <img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="/images/trebuchet/idea-sketch.webp" alt="First sketch of the game — a catapult lobbing a shot at a castle across a fixed range, with a note about playing cards" />
  </figure>
  <div class="proj-text">¶ <strong>Why it works as a two-player object.</strong> Splitting it into an attacker and a defender turns a single-player Flash game into a board game with a dynamic he hadn't seen anywhere else.</div>
</section>

¶ **The competitor search came back empty.** Nothing remotely similar existed as a physical game, which made the product entirely unique — worth stating plainly, because it's the strongest research finding in the project.

¶ **What the cardboard caught that the drawing didn't.** Where the structure needed extra support, and the aesthetic ratios of the form — things that only show up once the thing exists at size.

¶ **The Rhino friction.** Not knowing the exact thickness of the ply he'd be cutting made designing precision interlocking joints a headache; assembly was swift and fun, but working the joints out was the laborious part.

## Kerfing tests

¶ **Why kerfing.** A remarkably beautiful technique, and the one he most wanted to push while he had the chance — so the tests were as much curiosity as necessity.

  <div class="proj-text">¶ <strong>Only one pattern survived.</strong> The catapult needed a 90° bend, and that requirement eliminated every pattern but one.</div>
</section>

¶ **The winner was his own.** An off-set grid of cut-out ovals beat the recommended thin slits — an original pattern, and the one he finds most beautiful.

## Laser-cutting and assembly

¶ **The build itself went smoothly.** Wooden dowel for the axle, small spots of glue for the string and the handle — a short, satisfying assembly after the long CAD stretch.

<section class="proj-row">
  <figure class="proj-media">
    <img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="/images/trebuchet/ball-seat.webp" alt="The launch platform — a shallow concave dish rasterised into the plywood to seat the ball bearing" />
  </figure>
  <div class="proj-text">¶ <strong>The rasterised ball seat.</strong> Detuning the laser cutter to engrave a concave imprint into the wood, so the ball bearing self-centres and the shot flies straighter.</div>
</section>

## Rules and play testing

<section class="proj-row">
  <figure class="proj-media">

  </figure>
  <div class="proj-text">¶ <strong>What play-testing with the family changed.</strong> The action cards turned out to be a distraction rather than a mechanic, and the game was better without them.</div>
</section>

¶ **The rule that replaced them.** "Builder places one block, attacker launches two shots, repeat" — simpler, better to play, and easy enough for young children to pick up.

## Final thoughts

¶ **Lead-in.** A short line framing the scores below as an honest self-assessment.

- **Game design — 7/10** — ¶ Under-tested, but the attacker/defender dynamic is novel, and simple blocks give it near-endless replayability.
- **Object design and build quality — 8/10** — ¶ The catapult is the best thing here; family assumed he'd bought it. Against that: the base plates lack any detailing beyond numbering, and the last plate needed a guard to catch stray shots.
- **Presentation sheets — 6/10** — ¶ A big improvement on the drafts, but the faces still out-weigh the catapult, and colour coordination and composition hold them back.
- **Overall — 7/10** — ¶ The project as a whole is maybe a 5; the catapult alone pulls it to a 7.

¶ **Closing.** The mistakes were time management and divided attention — and he's still proud of it, because the catapult is beautiful.
